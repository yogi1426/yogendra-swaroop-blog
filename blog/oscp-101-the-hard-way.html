OSCP 101 — The Hard Way

My OSCP journey — the mindset, the grind, and what "Try Harder" actually means in practice.

Jan 4, 2020 7 min read
Offensive Security 101 — part 1 of 6
  1. OSCP 101 — The Hard Way
  2. Recon Like You Mean It — coming soon

Thank you for coming here. This post is about my experience in the OSCP journey — what I learned, doing hard work plus smart work, and trying harder.

"Every battle is won before it is fought." — Sun Tzu

Pre-Enrollment

Before you even enroll, spend time getting comfortable with the basics: networking fundamentals, Linux command line, and enough scripting to automate the boring parts. The labs punish people who skip this step.

The Basic Takeaways

  1. Buy the PWK/OSCP VIP membership — it'll boost your confidence and it's not too expensive in the long run.
  2. Don't switch off OSCP-like mindset in day-to-day work.
  3. Watch IppSec's videos on YouTube for lab walkthroughs.
  4. Do lab buffer overflow before taking the exam.
  5. Keep your weapons ready — remember one thing, "Every battle is won before it is fought."

Pre-Enrollment: The Mindset

One month before enrolling, I worked to prepare myself for the labs. I stayed up with my friends a little more to vent for the gaint and expensive course. Mostly, people ask me to go for advance labs directly. But hey, I am a warrior (yes, I like war). After discussing with my mentor, because he had cleared OSCP in 30% and tipped a great page, I decided to challenge myself this time.

Post-Enrollment: In the Labs

Post-enrollment would be all about OSCP labs and exam preparations, and keeping your cheatsheets ready. I finished the lab videos and PDF, and jumped into the labs. Well, I was not able to pwn even a single machine by the evening. I took a short break and came back, rooted 2 machines in the next few hours. I was happy.

I made a prime target of rooting at least two machines everyday for the first week, and trying at least 3 machines every day. I divided my slot of 5–5 hours, and then I fell sick because I had left everything and was spending too much time on the terminal. Even after falling sick I was still trying to pwn machines. After 2 days I was okay. I started again, and now working smartly — i.e. keeping health and labs balanced. I had rooted 15 boxes in the very first week, and that was quite a good progress.

Here, I would suggest that setting short targets is better, and in the labs you should know one day before what machines you are going to try the next day.

Time Between Labs Ending and Exam

What you should do when the labs have ended and your exam is a week later — revisit your notes, redo the buffer overflow, and make sure your report template is ready to go so you're not writing it under exam-week stress.

Exam Day

For what all challenges you should be prepared for — a calm mind, a solid methodology, and the discipline to step away from a box that isn't moving instead of tunnel-visioning for hours.

Closing Thoughts
